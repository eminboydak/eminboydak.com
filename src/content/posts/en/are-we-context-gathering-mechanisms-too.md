One day while working, a thought crossed my mind and I hit record with no plan whatsoever. At the three-minute mark I thought, "I'll stop right here"—then I thought again: a podcast? In three or four minutes? Nope, this needs at least fifteen or twenty. I liked that little decision I'd made all by myself so much that I kept going. Scattered as the ideas were, this spontaneous session is how it came out.

## The agent on my computer didn't know its own error

I have an AI agent on my computer that had been inactive for a while. I asked how it was doing, and it replied, "I'm ready to work and I'm right here." Yet this was the exact same agent that had stopped responding inside a group of agents. There was a bug in its system, but at the moment its memory woke back up, it had no idea; based on the context it held, it genuinely believed it was ready for duty.

When I told it about the error in the group, it had been completely oblivious up until that point. Suddenly, it needed a task, maybe even questioned its own purpose, gathered up all the context it had to trace where it left off, and through a bit of digging, found its way back to its last assignment.

I paused right there, because that looks a lot like us. We open our eyes to this world with a mission and make decisions by gathering context from everything around us.

> We act like a mechanism that makes decisions by gathering context from around us.

When you start looking at life like an observer, you realize you can actually change a great deal. Still, both our context and our memory are often incomplete; we just keep moving forward with our own story, unaware of what we're missing.

## Chasing the algorithm and adult ADHD

The algorithm, meanwhile, wasn't far from my mind either. Post Reels at 4:5, now 16:9 is coming—chasing aspect ratios gets exhausting, so I've stopped paying attention. Nothing monumental, just a personal conclusion of mine.

> Sometimes we feel like human lab rats trying to survive inside the algorithm.

Then again, it isn't easy to blame social media alone; we built this system ourselves as humanity. People crave dopamine by their own hand, and the media simply delivers it. Right in the middle of that loop, the true value of data emerges. Whether those dark scenarios where corporations hold more sway than governments will turn out as bleak as we imagine is up for debate, but where the data is flowing is obvious.

Amid all this mental clutter, having adult ADHD used to have sides I really disliked. Yet since workloads began shifting toward AI, I've started thinking that attention deficit isn't such a bad thing after all. Of course, AI isn't doing my entire job; I've integrated it into my own workflow. As a result, specializing in a single narrow niche—especially in software—isn't as viable as it used to be. Job descriptions and the way work gets done have changed.

The work was always there; what changed is that AI now carries the heavy part. What's left for us is thinking and deciding—and the more we think, the more we become people who think a lot on their own. And as you start thinking, you see that each of us lives a completely distinct life, like unique IDs on a blockchain. Even so, other people don't know us by the thoughts inside our heads, only by what we put out into the world. So instead of getting lost in endless options of "was it this, or was it that," I choose to put thought into action—to move my mouth and speak.

## On-device assistants in our pockets

The way we access information has shifted too. We used to reach knowledge by reading books; today we are heading toward a place where we can get things done with a single click. Everyone is moving in this direction—it's Apple that comes to mind first, so here's my example: alongside an on-device AI module at the chip level, Apple is opening the door to external models like Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini.

The real scenario, though, is this: as AI keeps shrinking until it can run in smaller and smaller places—right in our pockets—secure agents and interface controls come along with it, and what we end up with is an on-device AI assistant. What Siri has been trying to pull off for years is finally turning into reality, much like Jarvis. I still prefer typing on a keyboard, though plenty of people are already running small language models like Whisper on their machines and getting work done by voice. Thinking about shared home devices with facial recognition, like the new screen-equipped HomePod, communal digital interaction models inside families seem right around the corner. Of course, once you open up a shared device to AI so it recognizes the whole household, without solid security measures, that device could eventually turn into a psychological control machine for an entire family.

## Mango-flavored raisins

You could talk for hours about any of these topics, yet at the end of the day, one thing stands out: no matter how different our cultures are or how far apart we live in the world, we are similar creatures and the same thoughts cross our minds.

Toward the end of the recording, I happened to have some mango-flavored raisins in my hand. I'm guessing they dip the raisins in some mango-flavored liquid—though I have no idea, really, making assumptions about something I don't know—but they genuinely taste like mango. Since it's a snack marketed to kids, I assume they kept the ingredients as natural as possible—though in this system, nobody really knows what's inside a wrapper. Still, as an adult, I liked this kids' snack.
